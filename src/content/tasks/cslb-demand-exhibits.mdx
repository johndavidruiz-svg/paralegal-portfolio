whenUsed: "Compiled once a construction or home-improvement dispute reaches the demand-letter stage, before a CSLB complaint or civil suit is filed, to substantiate the contractor's breach and the homeowner's damages."
caJurisdictionNotes: "Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 7159, 7107, 7031; 16 CCR § 868 (CSLB complaint documentation)"

When a home-improvement dispute reaches the point of a formal demand, the letter itself is only as persuasive as the documents behind it. The paralegal assembles the exhibit packet — typically the signed home improvement contract, proof of payments made, dated photographs of the defective or incomplete work, any building department notice of violation or stop-work order, a CSLB license detail report confirming the contractor's status and bond, an independent licensed contractor's inspection and corrective-cost estimate, and the correspondence log documenting the breakdown in communication. Each exhibit is lettered sequentially (A, B, C…) rather than numbered, consistent with CSLB complaint intake practice, and cross-referenced against specific paragraphs of the demand letter so the attorney can cite directly to supporting evidence. The paralegal verifies that dates, dollar figures, and license numbers in the exhibit descriptions match the underlying documents exactly, since a demand letter's credibility — and its usefulness if the matter later proceeds to a CSLB complaint or civil suit — depends on the exhibits being accurate and internally consistent from the outset.
