## Fact pattern

Lisa Diaz, a homeowner in Fresno, California, hired Sierra Crest Construction, Inc. in January 2025 to remodel her kitchen and primary bathroom for a fixed contract price of $68,500. The written home improvement contract called for new plumbing rough-in, electrical circuits for updated appliances, custom cabinetry, and tile work, to be completed within twelve weeks. Sierra Crest began demolition in early February but never obtained the plumbing or electrical permits the scope of work required. By late May 2025, after Ms. Diaz had paid 90 percent of the contract price on the crew's representations of near-completion, Sierra Crest's principal stopped returning calls and texts, leaving the kitchen unusable and the bathroom stripped to the studs.

A neighbor's report of a possible gas smell brought a City of Fresno Building Division inspector to the property in June 2025, who red-tagged the unpermitted plumbing and electrical work and issued a notice of violation. Ms. Diaz retained counsel the following month. The paralegal pulled Sierra Crest's license history from the CSLB public license lookup, confirming the contractor's license remained active and was backed by the standard $25,000 disciplinary bond, and arranged for an independent licensed contractor to inspect the unfinished work. That inspection, completed in September 2025, itemized $46,800 in corrective repairs — regrading and re-permitting the plumbing, re-terminating exposed electrical runs, and re-securing cabinetry installed without adequate blocking. With the fact record assembled, the firm is sending a pre-litigation demand letter to Sierra Crest citing its statutory duties under the Contractors State License Law, concurrently with a complaint to the CSLB, and has organized the supporting documentation into a seven-exhibit packet (Exhibits A through G) to substantiate both.
